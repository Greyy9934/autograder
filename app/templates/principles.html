Design principles

The rules governing evidence, AI assistance, verification and grading.

← Back to the submission board
EVIDENCE AND RELIABILITY

Evidence over guesswork

  • Every reported finding should be traceable to evidence.
  • AI-generated answers are suggestions, not ground truth.
  • Reference solutions require instructor-approved specifications.
  • Generated tests do not prove correctness by themselves.
  • Execution failures and infrastructure failures are different.
  • Inconclusive evidence must never be silently counted as failure.
  • Complexity measurements are evidence, not mathematical proof.
  • Similarity flags are investigation signals, not proof of plagiarism.
SEPARATION OF RESPONSIBILITIES

AI reasoning is not deterministic verification

  • Each AI agent has a clearly defined responsibility and output.
  • The orchestrator coordinates work; it is not an AI agent.
  • Compilation, execution, comparison and scoring are deterministic software operations.
  • AI-generated reference implementations must be validated before being trusted as correctness oracles.
  • Mathematical proof, empirical performance and test outcomes remain separate forms of evidence.
  • The grading engine, not an AI model, controls the final score.
SAFETY AND FAIRNESS

Safe, explainable evaluation

  • Untrusted student programs must execute only inside an appropriately isolated environment.
  • Resource limits must apply to compilation and execution.
  • Infrastructure problems must not unfairly penalize a submission.
  • Missing evidence must be reported as missing, not invented.
  • Provisional results must be distinguished from final grades.
  • Every score should be explainable through its rubric and supporting evidence.
